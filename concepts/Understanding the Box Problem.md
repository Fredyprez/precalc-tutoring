## Understanding the Box Problem

### 1. Write the volume as a function of \(x\)

Find the box's length, width, and height after cutting out the corners and folding the sides upward. Think about how each cutout changes the original cardboard dimensions. Then use the formula \(V=LWH\) to express the volume in terms of \(x\).

### 2. Find a reasonable domain

The domain describes which values of \(x\) make sense for this situation. Since \(x\) represents the side length of each cutout, it must be positive. It also cannot be so large that the box's length or width becomes zero or negative.

### 3. Maximize the volume

Use a graphing utility, such as Desmos, to graph your volume function over the reasonable domain. Find the highest point on the graph.

- The **\(x\)-coordinate** tells you the cutout size that produces the maximum volume.
- The **\(y\)-coordinate** tells you the maximum volume of the box.

Remember, the goal is to find the cutout size that makes the box hold the most—not the largest possible cutout.
